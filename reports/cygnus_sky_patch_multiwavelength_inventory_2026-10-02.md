# Multiwavelength observations of the project's Cygnus sky patch

Literature and archive-documentation review, 2 October 2026.

## Spatial scope and reading the inventory

The primary region is the **sky rectangle 77° ≤ Galactic longitude l ≤ 83°, −1.5° ≤ Galactic latitude b ≤ +4°**, approximately **33 square degrees**. This is the census footprint defined in [the project's manuscript](../manuscript/main.tex), not a boundary around a particular association. The wider project rectangle, **72° ≤ l ≤ 88°, −5° ≤ b ≤ +8°**, provides context for large structures and runaway searches; it is not the footprint for which this report assesses primary coverage.

Selection is by **position**, without imposing the Gaia sample's parallax, magnitude, or membership cuts. Gas, dust, stars, background sources, remnants, binaries, and diffuse emission are relevant if their observed footprints intersect this patch. Names such as Cygnus X, Cygnus OB2, DR21, γ Cygni, and the Cygnus cocoon identify published fields or features; they do not imply that all emission belongs to one object or lies at one distance.

This is a broad **survey- and observing-program inventory**, based on primary papers and observatory/archive documentation. It includes major legacy datasets, recent surveys, and important pointed campaigns. It is **not an exhaustive export of every observation ID**, nor a pixel-by-pixel audit of archive holdings. No science images, event lists, or cubes were downloaded or reanalysed for this review. The remaining archive-level completeness checks are specified below.

Coverage labels in the tables:

- **Full nominal:** the published survey footprint includes the rectangle; exposure, masks, failed fields, saturation, and release completeness still need checking.
- **Partial:** a documented mosaic or strip intersects the rectangle but does not cover all of it. Quoted survey areas include any portions outside our rectangle.
- **Pointed:** small fields or individual sources within the rectangle; not representative sampling of the whole patch.
- **Wide:** an instrument observes the patch together with a much larger surrounding region; source extraction and background modelling require that context.

Dates are **observation dates** when established. “Published YYYY; epoch not extracted” deliberately identifies a remaining metadata gap rather than substituting publication year for observing year. Angular resolutions are approximate and product-dependent. Source counts refer to the **published survey**, not a new count inside our rectangle. Where no local scientific result was checked, the final column explicitly describes **products/use**, not a claimed discovery.

## What these observations already establish for the patch

The sky region has extensive coverage from radio to PeV gamma rays, but **uniformity varies more than the list of mission names suggests**. All-sky/northern-sky products supply a broad foundation; much of the most informative molecular spectroscopy, infrared mapping and sensitive X-ray imaging covers selected clouds or small central fields. The quoted footprints show that these mosaics cannot simply be treated as observations of the entire Gaia census box.

The molecular data provide the strongest immediate warning about projection: the [MWISP/Gaia analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00061v1) finds several layers along these sightlines. Dust emission, ionized gas and gamma-ray templates should therefore initially retain their angular and, where available, velocity information. Converting every flux or angular scale using one association distance would erase the distinction the inventory is intended to preserve.

The high-energy results also describe **different angular and temporal components**: stellar X-ray point sources, diffuse thermal plasma, a known remnant, compact binaries, and very extended gamma-ray emission. The [LAT component analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04504), [2017 binary detections](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05271), and [LHAASO bubble analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10100v4) make those distinctions explicit. Their spectra are not automatically measurements of one object through different instruments.

Consequently, none of a dust cavity, a molecular wing, a diffuse X-ray component, a ²⁶Al excess or an extended gamma-ray source alone supplies a count or date of supernovae associated with the Gaia population. They provide complementary constraints once spatial overlap, distance, velocities, source decomposition and selection effects have been assessed.

## Data acquisition priorities and reproducible completion of the census

The following ordering is a proposed use of the inventory, not an analysis already performed.

1. **Build a uniform angular baseline:** CGPS continuum/H I, HI4PI, Planck, IRAS/AKARI/WISE, IGAPS images, 2MASS/UKIDSS/Pan-STARRS catalogues, ROSAT exposure/intensity maps and LAT events/exposure. Keep each native footprint and uncertainty map.
2. **Add velocity information:** obtain the MWISP regional CO cubes and establish their access conditions; supplement with available FCRAO, Nobeyama, JCMT, GLOSTAR, WHAM and SOFIA products. Retain distinct velocity components before proposing distance associations.
3. **Add partial high-resolution layers:** Spitzer, Herschel, Bolocam/MAMBO, Chandra/XMM, GMRT and pointed interferometric data. Map precisely which fraction of the census rectangle has each measurement. Use images as well as catalogues where nebulosity or crowding matters.
4. **Model high-energy observations in their own valid regions:** preserve the wider surrounding field for diffuse backgrounds and broad emission; separate steady extended structures from variable sources. Compare fluxes only after reconciling spatial templates, point-spread functions, energy bands and observing epochs.
5. **Only then test connections to the Gaia groups:** position, parallax/extinction constraints, gas velocities, stellar radial velocities and age information can inform association. Keep alternatives when several layers fit the same projected structure.

To turn this document into a demonstrably exhaustive **archive observation inventory**, query spatial intersections with the Galactic rectangle, not only target names or pointing centres. For services requiring ICRS polygons, transform a sampled rectangle boundary rather than treating Galactic longitude/latitude as equatorial coordinates. Include observations whose field of view intersects the boundary even when their pointing centre lies outside it. Use a separate query and coverage mask for the wider project box.

For every returned observation/product, retain: archive and observation ID; programme; instrument/band or line; start/end time; actual footprint; usable exposure; angular/spectral resolution; calibration/release version; access status; image/cube/event/catalogue type; and the source URL/DOI. Distinguish **public calibrated products**, **public raw observations needing reduction**, and **literature-only products whose availability is unconfirmed**. An abstract or catalogue entry does not certify public access to the associated image or cube.

Useful starting points are [IRSA](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/) for infrared/microwave surveys, [CADC CGPS](https://www2.cadc-ccda.hia-iha.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/en/cgps/) for radio maps/cubes, [NRAO](https://data.nrao.edu/) for VLA observations, [MAST](https://mast.stsci.edu/) for UV/optical/space-IR pointings, [HEASARC](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/) for high-energy archives, and [Fermi SSC](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/) for LAT events and response information. Instrument papers linked in the tables identify specialised products not necessarily exposed through a common archive.

This document therefore supplies a literature-backed observing landscape and a concrete acquisition plan. **The remaining completeness boundary is the exposure-level archive query and footprint audit**, particularly for pointed observations, ultraviolet coverage, recent releases and privately reduced molecular-line mosaics.
