# WP0 — Deduplication and novelty report

**Search date:** 2026-07-20  
**Expanded coverage window:** 2024-01-01 through 2026-07-20, plus all-time exact/near-exact searches  
**Verdict:** **No direct duplicate identified. One relevant Gaia DR3 partial overlap was found and must be cited.**

## 1. What counted as a direct duplicate

A paper was treated as a direct duplicate only if it combined most of the following for Cyg OB2/Cygnus:

- Gaia-era membership and distance treatment;
- stellar extinction/luminosity/mass inference;
- IMF normalization or an equivalent demographic reconstruction;
- a history or posterior for past SNe / last-SN timing; and
- an explicit test of the proposed Cyg OB2 gamma-ray/SNR interpretation or an equivalent demographic test.

Papers about Cygnus gamma-ray transport, wind energetics, generic SNR modeling, or Gaia distance structure were logged as adjacent prior art, not duplicates.

## 4. Novelty verdict

**No direct duplicate identified as of 2026-07-20 after the expanded 2024–2026 search.** The planned Paper 1 remains differentiated by the combination of:

- Gaia DR3 probabilistic membership and line-of-sight subgroup assignment;
- per-star extinction and stellar-mass inference;
- an explicit completeness-calibrated IMF likelihood;
- a stochastic mapping from surviving stars to all deaths and successful SNe;
- age-duration, isochrone, IMF, binary, runaway, and failed-SN sensitivity branches; and
- a joint demographic plausibility test of the H25 Cyg OB2 event hypothesis.

The strongest competitive risk is now explicit: Paíz et al. (2025) is a Gaia DR3 regional cluster/member catalogue and should be cited as the closest data-level precedent. B19/B20 provide the earlier Gaia-era and spectroscopic ingredients, Menchiari and H25 address the Cygnus physical environment, and recent 2025–2026 papers revisit gamma-ray explanations. The manuscript should therefore state the novelty as an integrated demographic test, not as the first Gaia study of Cyg OB2.

## 5. Residual limitations and reproducibility note

- ADS’s public web interface was not fully machine-exportable in this environment. The citation/title searches were used for discovery, and the relevant papers were cross-checked against arXiv, publisher, or CDS/VizieR records. This report is a dated reproducible search snapshot, not a proof that an unindexed preprint, thesis, or conference abstract cannot exist.
- Conference programs and proceedings can lag arXiv indexing. Search ICRC/TeVPA again immediately before submission.
- A 2025 Gaia DR3 catalogue paper can be missed by a narrow “supernova history” query; this is why the expanded search also used `stellar population`, `open cluster`, `member`, `IMF`, and `missing stars` terms.
- The execution plan’s Menchiari identifier was corrected from `2306.00946` to `2402.07784` for this audit.
- Before claiming priority in a paper, rerun the searches with the final title/keywords and inspect citations to H25, B19, B20, W15, and Martin 2010 through ADS.
