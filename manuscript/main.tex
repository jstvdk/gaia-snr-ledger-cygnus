\abstract
% context
{The supernova history of a young association sets the mechanical energy
driving its superbubble, its compact-remnant population, its radioactive-isotope
budget, and the viability of any interpretation of its high-energy emission
that invokes a recent explosion. It is hard to measure because it couples
membership, extinction, age, completeness and high-mass stellar evolution, and
existing numbers for Cygnus~OB2 are population-synthesis estimates built on
heterogeneous literature inputs.}
% aims
{We reconstruct the massive-star and supernova history of Cygnus~OB2 from a
single homogeneous census, and separate the conclusions the data control from
those the carried model choices control.}
% methods
{From \textit{Gaia}~DR3 astrometry and photometry joined to 2MASS and to a
literature spectroscopic census we build a probabilistic member list, decompose
it kinematically, fit per-star extinctions against a spectroscopically anchored
kriged spatial prior, infer subgroup ages and per-star masses from two isochrone
families, and normalize the initial mass function by a response-aware Poisson
fit to star counts between 2 and 8\,$M_\odot$. The normalization is tested out
of sample against the observed census above 8\,$M_\odot$. Supernovae are counted
by stellar-evolution turnoff in a Monte Carlo over $2\times10^{6}$ population
realizations per branch. Isochrone family, extinction law, IMF slope,
star-formation duration and explodability are carried as explicit branches and
never averaged.}
% results
{Cygnus~OB2 resolves into three stable kinematic subgroups of \ageA, \ageB\ and
\ageC\,Myr, two older and one younger, with a systematic age envelope far wider
than those three central values. Conditional on the permissive all-explode
prescription, the baseline gives $N_{\rm SN} = \NSN$ (median \NSNmedian, 68\%
[\NSNlo, \NSNhi]) and the \NSNheadbranches\ headline branches span
\NSNheadlo--\NSNheadhi; the model-branch spread exceeds the stochastic interval
of any one branch. The corresponding probability that the most recent event
falls within 100\,kyr is \Precent\ on the baseline and spans
\Precentheadlo--\Precentheadhi. Every inferred stellar death lies above
$\progenitorFloorLo\,M_\odot$, so the ledger measures very-massive-star
explodability rather than the canonical regime. Association-wide closure is near
Salpeter, but the subgroups are heterogeneous: their closing slopes span
\closingAlphaC--\closingAlphaA.}
% conclusions
{The stellar census alone does not decide whether these very massive deaths made
supernovae: a hard direct-collapse cutoff at or below $\bhSafeCut\,M_\odot$
returns zero. PSR~J2032+4127 independently excludes an entirely explosion-free
history if its association with Cygnus~OB2 is accepted. The combined evidence
therefore requires either a successful explosion in the very-massive regime or
an older or binary-stripped progenitor channel absent from the coeval
single-star ledger. The
supernova-driven interpretation of the Cygnus $\gamma$-ray cocoon is evaluated
as a conditional scenario-availability score rather than a calibrated
probability; it is controlled by the high-mass IMF slope and by two terms that
are a model mapping and a bound rather than measurements. \textit{Gaia}~DR4 will
sharpen ages and runaways but not the high-mass IMF; a COSI measurement or upper
limit would constrain the joint IMF and very-massive-star yield problem.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Why young-association supernova histories matter}

How many stars an association has already lost, and when, is a population
property that several distinct fields depend on. It sets the mechanical energy
injected into the surrounding superbubble and therefore the structure of the
cavity; it sets the expected number of compact remnants; it sets the supernova
contribution to the radioactive $^{26}$Al and $^{60}$Fe budgets
\citep{knodlseder2002, martin2010}; and it decides whether interpretations of
high-energy emission that require a recent explosion are admissible at all.

That last dependence has become acute for Cygnus~OB2. The $\gamma$-ray cocoon
around it, detected from GeV to hundreds of TeV, is the clearest Galactic
PeVatron candidate, and the proposed accelerators span the association's
collective winds, a supernova exploding into the cavity those winds have carved
\citep{harer2025, suppresseddiffusion2026}, and --- since LHAASO's detection of
a variable ultra-high-energy source coincident with Cygnus~X-3 --- a microquasar
\citep{cygx3pevatron2026}. The field also hosts the $\gamma$~Cygni supernova
remnant and the pulsar PSR~J2032+4127 with its wind nebula. These candidates are
not mutually exclusive, but they place different demands on the stellar
population, and the supernova-driven class places the sharpest one:
the fiducial model of \citet{harer2025} uses a powerful Type~Ic event in the
cavity at roughly 50\,kyr, with an energy of a few $10^{51}$\,erg. Those are
model-dependent requirements rather than direct observables: their permitted
age and energy change with the assumed cavity density, acceleration efficiency
and particle cut-off.

\subsection{Why the measurement is hard}

An association's present-day census is not its initial population, and the
mapping between them runs through every step of the analysis. Extinction in
Cygnus is severe and differential, so a single regional value biases the
inferred masses. Membership must be probabilistic, because a bright O star with
poor astrometry cannot be allowed to fall out silently. Completeness is a
function of both mass and sky position, so a scalar correction is not the right
object. The sampling of the upper mass function is stochastic at these numbers.
And the two quantities that convert a census into a death toll --- the age and
the fate of the most massive stars --- are precisely the two the data constrain
least well.

\subsection{Why Cygnus~OB2}

Cygnus~OB2 is one of the most massive young associations in the Galaxy, with
tens of O stars, three Wolf--Rayet stars and a young pulsar
\citep{wright2015, berlanas2020}. It has a rich enough massive-star population
for the mass function to be measured rather than assumed, severe enough
differential extinction to make the extinction treatment load-bearing,
substructure that lets the assumption of coevality be tested rather than
imposed, and independent supernova markers that can be compared against a ledger
built without reference to them.

\subsection{What previous estimates could and could not do}

Prior estimates disagree in ways that trace to their inputs rather than to their
methods. \citet{knodlseder2002} and \citet{martin2010} modelled the Cygnus
complex as a whole from heterogeneous literature counts and assumed ages;
\citet{menchiari2024} adopt a fixed association mass and predict $7\pm2.5$
supernovae at 3\,Myr and $26\pm5$ at 5\,Myr --- a factor of four spanned by a
2\,Myr change in an assumed age. The lesson we take from this is not that those
numbers are wrong but that a supernova count is only interpretable together with
the machinery that says what it is sensitive to.

\subsection{What this paper adds}

The stellar side of the problem is tractable. If the present-day mass function is
measured where it is complete, the initial mass function can be normalized there
and extrapolated above the turnoff, and the stars missing from the upper main
sequence are the ones that have died. This is the deficit method of
\citet{fuchs2006} and \citet{zucker2022}, and \textit{Gaia}~DR3 makes it
applicable to Cygnus~OB2 with membership, distance and extinction all derived
from the same homogeneous data set.

The methodological contribution is that the whole chain is internally linked,
branch-resolved and provenance-controlled: the isochrones that set the
extinctions are the isochrones that set the ages; the completeness response is
measured by injection through the actual selection rather than assumed; the
normalization is tested out of sample; and every discrete modelling choice is
carried as a branch to the end rather than marginalized into a single number.
Figure~\ref{fig:flow} shows the chain, marks which nodes are observations and
which are inferences, and marks where a failed validation test or a one-sided
bound enters.

The cocoon and radioactive-isotope questions then appear as applications of that
ledger --- demonstrations of what it can and cannot decide --- rather than as
the premise of the work. Three choices govern everything and are stated here
rather than buried: discrete modelling choices are \emph{carried as branches and
never averaged}; every comparison against an independent supernova marker was
\emph{pre-registered} before it was computed; and results that failed are
reported as failed.

This conditional structure is the scientific result rather than an absence of
one. The census identifies which stars could already have died, the branch grid
quantifies the supernova history if those deaths explode, and the pulsar then
excludes the all-zero outcome under the association hypothesis. What remains is
a physical alternative: either at least one star in the very-massive regime
exploded successfully, or the pulsar came through an older or binary-modified
channel not represented by the coeval single-star calculation. The analysis
therefore converts a poorly localized uncertainty in ``the supernova number''
into a testable constraint on high-mass evolution.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{\textit{Gaia}~DR3 and 2MASS}

Two \textit{Gaia}~DR3 queries define the working samples: a narrow box
($l \in [77\degr, 83\degr]$, $b \in [-1\fdg5, +4\degr]$) for the census, and a
wide box ($l \in [72\degr, 88\degr]$, $b \in [-5\degr, +8\degr]$) for runaway
traceback. Parallaxes were selected in $[0.35, 1.1]$\,mas and $G < 19$ ---
deliberately generous, because the selection is refined by clustering rather
than by cuts. Parallax zero-points follow \citet{lindegren2021}. Near-infrared
photometry comes from the official \textit{Gaia}~$\times$~2MASS cross-match.
Table~\ref{tab:subgroups} summarizes the resulting subgroups.

\subsection{Spectroscopic anchors}

The brightest O stars have unreliable or missing \textit{Gaia} astrometry, so
they cannot be allowed to fall out of the census silently. A merged anchor table
carries spectral types and, where available, effective temperatures from
\citet{wright2015}, \citet{berlanas2019, berlanas2020} and the Galactic O-Star
Catalog. Source identities and literature-evidence rows are distinguished
throughout: the anchor table resolves to fewer unique \textit{Gaia} sources than
it has literature rows, because several stars are catalogued more than once. The
spectral reclassification of the association's B0 population by
\citet{fastrotators2025} postdates our freeze and should be folded in at the DR4
rerun.

Anchors are exempt from the astrometric quality cuts and are treated
individually. That exemption is a deliberate methodological choice with a
measurable cost, and Sect.~\ref{sec:membership-result} reports its size rather
than absorbing it.

\subsection{Independent supernova markers}

PSR~J2032+4127 from the ATNF catalogue, $\gamma$~Cygni (G78.2+2.1) from Green's
supernova-remnant catalogue, and the INTEGRAL $^{26}$Al measurements of the
Cygnus region were frozen at the data-acquisition stage, \emph{before} any
ledger existed, and their comparison statistics were fixed in a pre-registration
written before the ledger was run. The $^{60}$Fe forecast of
Sect.~\ref{sec:cosi} is \emph{not} in this category: it was chosen after the
ledger existed. The two are kept apart everywhere they are used.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Membership selection}\label{sec:membership}

Association membership is decided by a two-component cluster-versus-field
Gaussian mixture in five features: Galactic position relative to the field
centre, the zero-point-corrected parallax, and the two proper-motion components.
A DBSCAN pass over the same space provides the training seed only; it assigns no
final label. Membership probabilities come from a Monte Carlo over each star's
full \textit{Gaia} astrometric covariance, so a star with large but consistent
errors is treated as uncertain rather than as excluded. The mixture prior was
calibrated against three control fields at matched Galactic latitude processed
through the identical pipeline. The mixture publishes a continuous probability,
but a finite catalogue is needed by the photometric and age pipeline. We use
$P>0.5$ for that handoff: for a two-class posterior it is the maximum-posterior
decision rule, equivalent to requiring that membership be favoured over the
field under equal misclassification costs. It is an operational choice, not a
universal astrophysical threshold. Inside the selected catalogue the continuous
probability is retained and used wherever the downstream estimator calls for a
membership weight; spectroscopic anchors that fail the astrometric quality cuts
enter as explicitly flagged manual exceptions.

The threshold is checked rather than justified by convention. Moving it from
0.4 to 0.6 changes the clean automatic catalogue from
\thresholdMembersLow--\thresholdMembersHigh\ stars. Across that interval the
automatic recovery of the external membership list changes only from
\thresholdRecallLow\% to \thresholdRecallHigh\%, while the mean control-to-target
yield falls from \thresholdControlLow\% to \thresholdControlHigh\%. The adopted
0.5 boundary retains \thresholdMembersAdopted\ automatic members, with
\thresholdRecallAdopted\% automatic recovery and a \thresholdControlAdopted\%
mean control yield (Appendix~\ref{app:membership}). A stricter cut buys purity at
the cost of a smaller calibration sample; a looser cut does the reverse.

Two limitations follow directly and are carried to the end. First, because the
control fields calibrated the prior, their measured yield is not a wholly
external false-positive rate. Second, because parallax is a selection feature,
any test for structure along the line of sight is conditional on the sample the
selection produced (Sect.~\ref{sec:distance}).

\subsection{Kinematic subgroups}\label{sec:subgroups}

Subgroup decomposition is a \emph{separate} operation on the clean automatic
member sample, in a \emph{four}-dimensional feature space
$(l, b, \mu_{\alpha*}, \mu_\delta)$ from which parallax is deliberately
excluded. The exclusion belongs here and not at the membership stage: the
members form a single population at $\ourDistance$\,kpc with an intrinsic depth
of order 45\,pc, which DR3 parallaxes cannot resolve. Admitting parallax as a
clustering feature could therefore make the recovered labels primarily reflect
measurement noise and the preceding selection rather than physical depth.

The number of components was accepted on seed stability rather than on an
information criterion; only $k = 3$ is stable across 50 deterministic seeds, and
per-star label confusion over those seeds is below a fraction of a per cent.
HDBSCAN was scanned for completeness but not used for labels. Anchors that carry
no astrometric label remain unassigned and are carried for provenance.

\subsection{The distance test and its exact scope}\label{sec:distance}

Within the selected member sample we compare a one-component and a
two-component distance model and find no support for the second. We state the
limit of that non-confirmation precisely: parallax was a selection feature, so a
genuine foreground group would have been removed before the test ran. The
defensible claim is that \emph{there is no evidence for two distances among the
members we select}, not that the foreground population of \citet{berlanas2019}
does not exist. A parallax-blind membership rerun is the only way to settle it;
it is deferred because it requires re-deriving the entire downstream chain on a
different member sample, which is a second paper's worth of work rather than a
robustness check.

\subsection{Extinction}\label{sec:extinction}

Differential extinction is the Cygnus problem, and a single regional $A_V$ is
inadmissible. Anchors receive $A_V$ from fixed-$T_{\rm eff}$ intrinsic colours,
which avoids the temperature--extinction degeneracy that broadband photometry
suffers for hot stars. All other members are fitted against reddened synthetic
photometry from the same isochrone families used for the ages, in six bands with
missing bands masked rather than imputed, under an anchor-informed spatial
prior.

That prior's mean is a simple-kriging estimate over the eight nearest anchors
using a variogram fitted to the anchors themselves, and its width is the
unconditional variogram sigma. This replaced an earlier construction whose two
moments contradicted each other: it widened the prior for stars far from any
anchor, correctly declaring those anchors uninformative, while still centring on
them at full weight. The repair matters for one subgroup only, and for an
observational reason rather than a modelling one --- Cyg~OB2-B's local anchor
support is far sparser than its siblings' (Fig.~\ref{fig:extinction}).

One systematic is carried rather than removed: at matched sky position,
prior-free broadband photometry prefers an $A_V$ about half a magnitude below
the spectroscopically calibrated anchors. The absolute extinction scale, and
hence the mass scale and $N_{\rm SN}$, therefore rests on the anchor
calibration.

\subsection{Ages and masses}\label{sec:ages}

PARSEC and MIST are carried in parallel from the start, never merged. Two age
indicators are used, an upper-main-sequence fit and a counts-based fit, and both
are retained. Of the age branches attempted a minority are excluded outright or
rail against the grid edge, and those are reported rather than dropped
(Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}). Isochrones are interpolated between native
table ages in log space rather than snapped to the nearest tabulated age,
because a stepped turnoff would make $N_{\rm SN}$ a step function of age.

The upper-main-sequence ages (\ageumsA, \ageumsB, \ageumsC\,Myr) are
\emph{the prior}, not the adopted values. What the ledger consumes is the
counts-based age of Sect.~\ref{sec:imf}, which is inferred jointly with the
normalization because the two are correlated through the fit. Only Cyg~OB2-B
moves materially between the two definitions, by $+\ageShiftB$\,Myr, and that
shift is worth a factor \snRatioB\ in B's supernova count --- which is why both
are reported and labelled (Fig.~\ref{fig:ages}).

B's counts-based posterior concentrates \railB\% of its weight on the topmost of
its nine prior nodes. A posterior that piles up against the edge of its prior
grid reports a bound, not a measurement, and the direction is known: older means
a lower turnoff means \emph{more} supernovae. B's contribution to $N_{\rm SN}$
is therefore a one-sided lower bound, and Cyg~OB2-C sits on a family-dependent
boundary at which its turnoff crosses the IMF's upper limit.

\subsection{Completeness and IMF normalization}\label{sec:imf}

The normalization $k$ is fitted per subgroup by a Poisson likelihood over mass
bins between 2 and 8\,$M_\odot$, against a forward model that convolves the
assumed IMF with a completeness response measured by injecting synthetic stars
into the real field and recovering them through the actual selection, end to
end at catalogue level. The response, not a scalar completeness, is the correct
object: the mass estimator has 22--25\% scatter, so it moves stars across bin
edges and across both window boundaries. No subgroup reaches an absolute 95\%
completeness edge, which is why a completeness cut was never an option.

The truth-side age is marginalized jointly with $k$ over nine age nodes drawn
from the CMD posterior and reweighted by the Poisson likelihood of the observed
counts with $k$ integrated out analytically. The binary fraction is
mass-dependent in the final version of the injection chain.

A branch passes the residual-fit criterion only if its $\chi^2$ probability, its residual
trend probability and its worst absolute Pearson residual are all inside their
thresholds, in all three subgroups. Sect.~\ref{sec:massfunction} reports what
that validation actually returns, which is not what a reader would infer from the
baseline alone. Failed branches are retained as explicit sensitivity cases and
never deleted, because deleting a branch because it fits badly under one
assumption is a way of selecting the assumption.

\subsection{Massive-star closure}\label{sec:closure}

The closure test compares the number of living stars above 8\,$M_\odot$
predicted by the normalization against the number observed. It is
\emph{out of sample}: $k$ is fitted from 2--8\,$M_\odot$ counts and the
$>8\,M_\odot$ census never enters that likelihood. Both sides are computed with
the same forward response, integrated with no lower cut-off, because the
observed side counts every member's $P(M > 8\,M_\odot)$ whatever its true mass;
truncating that integral at the threshold the response smears across was a real
defect in an earlier version and is recorded as such
(Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}). The observed side combines members, orphan
anchors inside the footprint but outside the member sample, and
false-positive-corrected runaways.

Following the convention of the underlying work, the closure ratio is
\emph{observed over predicted}, so a value above unity means the census contains
more massive stars than the extrapolated normalization predicts.

\subsection{Runaways}\label{sec:runaways}

Runaways are recovered from the wide box by tracing \emph{peculiar} proper
motions backwards over the subgroup age windows, with the association's systemic
motion subtracted --- Cygnus~OB2's bulk motion is larger than a typical ejection
signature, so absolute proper motions measure drift rather than ejection. The
false-positive rate is measured per separation bin from control fields, in the
same spirit as recent \textit{Gaia} runaway searches in other associations
\citep{velob1runaways2026}.

The recovered count is a lower bound twice over: the traceback is
two-dimensional, so line-of-sight ejections are missed, and the finite wide box
caps recovery at a velocity--time ceiling. Runaways do \emph{not} enter
$N_{\rm SN}$. They are living stars below the turnoff, so they change neither
$k$, which is fitted below 8\,$M_\odot$, nor the turnoff mass. What they bound is
location, not number --- and applying a fraction measured on living, lower-mass
stars to dead, more massive progenitors is an assumption, not a measurement
(Sect.~\ref{sec:cocoon}).

\subsection{The supernova ledger}\label{sec:ledger}

For each branch and each of $2\times10^{6}$ iterations we draw a paired
$(k, \mathrm{age})$ sample from the joint posterior --- paired, because the two
are correlated through the fit --- draw a star-formation duration, sample a
discrete stellar population from the IMF conditioned on the calibration-window
counts, and ask which stars have passed the turnoff. The stellar lifetime is
obtained by inverting the project's own turnoff relation, so the ledger and the
ages cannot disagree about when a star of a given mass dies. A death becomes a
supernova only if it satisfies the explodability branch.

Two explodability prescriptions are carried. The first is \emph{all explode}.
The second is a \emph{hard direct-collapse cutoff}, in which no star above a
fixed mass produces a supernova; the scan over that threshold, not the single
adopted value, is the primary explodability result
(Sect.~\ref{sec:explodability}). We emphasise that this is a hard cutoff and not
an implementation of the non-monotonic explodability maps of
\citet{sukhbold2016} and \citet{ertl2016}; no detailed high-mass explodability
prescription was implemented here.

\subsection{Branches, and what is never averaged}\label{sec:branches}

The branch grid carries five modelling decisions, each for a different physical
reason.

\begin{description}
\item[Stellar-evolution family:] PARSEC and MIST are carried separately because
their high-mass turnoffs and colour--magnitude relations are independent model
calibrations. Their difference is a model systematic, not a probability
distribution to average.
\item[Extinction law:] $R_V \in \{3.0,3.1,3.5\}$ spans the canonical diffuse-ISM
value and the plausible range across the Cygnus sightline. The same value is
used consistently when deriving extinction, age, mass and completeness.
\item[IMF slope:] $\alpha \in \{2.0,2.3,2.6\}$ brackets a shallow high-mass
population, a Salpeter-like reference, and a steep sensitivity case. This is the
axis to which both the massive-star closure and the supernova count are most
sensitive.
\item[Formation duration:] $\delta \in \{0,1,2\}$\,Myr compares a coeval
population with modest internal age spreads. A wider duration is not carried
because it would cease to be a perturbation around the independently inferred
subgroup ages.
\item[Explodability:] the permissive all-explode prescription counts every
stellar death as a supernova. The hard direct-collapse scan instead asks how the
answer changes if stars above a threshold fail. The latter is a diagnostic
family of cutoffs, not a probability-weighted physical explodability model.
\end{description}

The baseline branch is PARSEC, $R_V=3.1$, $\alpha=2.3$, $\delta=0$, all explode.
It is a transparent reference coordinate: the canonical extinction law,
Salpeter-like slope, minimal coeval assumption and maximally permissive
explodability prescription. It is not asserted to be more probable than the
other branches. The full tabulated grid contains $2\times3\times3\times3=54$
all-explode branches; each can then be re-evaluated against the direct-collapse
threshold scan. No probability weights exist for these discrete choices, so
they are never averaged into a pseudo-posterior.

The \emph{headline} all-explode branch set excludes $\alpha = 2.6$. The
criterion is the calibration-window $\chi^2$, an internal fit statistic fixed long before the
ledger existed, on which $\alpha = 2.6$ is the best fit in \alphaSixWins\ of
\alphaCells\ cells and has the worst median ($\chi^2 = \alphaSixChi$ against
\alphaThreeChi\ for $\alpha = 2.3$). The census closure agrees independently but
was deliberately \emph{not} used to make this choice, so that it remains
available as an out-of-sample validation of the extrapolation
(Sect.~\ref{sec:closure-result}). $\alpha = 2.6$ is reported throughout with its
own numbers ($N_{\rm SN} = \NSNdroplo$--\NSNdrophi) and is never deleted.

The headline set is selected on $\alpha$ and on nothing else. In particular it
is \emph{not} selected on mass-function fit quality, and
Sect.~\ref{sec:massfunction} reports how many of its branches rest on fits that
passed their own fit-quality criterion.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Membership, contamination and kinematic
substructure}\label{sec:membership-result}

Of \NmembersAll{} catalogue rows, \Nmembers{} have $P > 0.5$ and \Nlabelled{}
carry a subgroup label: Cyg~OB2-A (\NsubA), Cyg~OB2-B (\NsubB) and Cyg~OB2-C
(\NsubC). The three subgroups are stable in the sense defined in
Sect.~\ref{sec:subgroups} and are recovered with the same membership at every
seed.

The recall benchmark against \citet{berlanas2019} needs stating in two parts

\subsection{Differential extinction and subgroup age
structure}\label{sec:sfh}

Extinction is spatially coherent and cannot be represented by a single regional
value; the median $A_V$ differs between subgroups by more than a magnitude
(Table~\ref{tab:subgroups}). Cygnus~OB2 is not coeval. Its three subgroups have
counts-based ages of \ageA, \ageB\ and \ageC\,Myr --- \textbf{two older
subgroups and one younger} --- with a spread of \ageSpread\,Myr between the
central values. This differs from the structure the same pipeline returned
before the extinction prior was corrected, which read as one older subgroup and
two younger; the change followed a pre-registered fix whose four predictions
were confirmed in advance.

\textbf{The spread between three central values is not the age uncertainty.}
The envelope across both retained age indicators is 2.25--5.67\,Myr, and that is
what everything downstream inherits (Fig.~\ref{fig:ages}). Within it, B's
posterior rails against its prior grid and is a one-sided bound, and C's age is
family-dependent in a way that moves it across the boundary discussed in
Sect.~\ref{sec:ledger-result}.

\subsection{The living massive-star census and subgroup-dependent
closure}\label{sec:closure-result}

The strongest objection to any deficit method is that the IMF slope is
constrained where the counts are complete and used where they are not. Here that
objection has an answer, because the closure test never entered the fit.

The living census totals \livingTotal\ stars above 8\,$M_\odot$:
\livingMembers\ in the member channel, \livingOrphans\ spectroscopic anchors
inside the footprint but outside the member sample, and \runawaysBinned\
runaways. The runaway search recovers \runawaysRaw\ raw candidates,
\runawaysCorrected\ after the measured false-positive correction, and it
recovers the canonical ejected star BD$+$43\degr3654 at unit probability with
$38.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and a 1.36\,Myr look-back time against literature values of
$\sim$40\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and 1.6\,Myr --- from an estimator that knows nothing
about the published answer.

\paragraph{Association-wide closure is near Salpeter, but the subgroups differ.}
Aggregated over the grid at $\alpha = 2.3$, observed and predicted counts above
8\,$M_\odot$ agree at the few-to-fifteen per cent level, and \emph{which} number
one quotes depends on the aggregation: the median of the \closureCells\ subgroup
ratios is \closureMedianRatios, while the star-weighted ratio of summed observed
to summed predicted counts is \closureWeighted. We report both because they are
different quantities and because the difference is itself informative --- the
subgroups carry very different numbers of observed massive stars.

The residual is emphatically not uniform (Fig.~\ref{fig:closure}). At
$\alpha = 2.3$ the grid-median closure ratios are \closureGridA\ for A,
\closureGridB\ for B and \closureGridC\ for C --- on the baseline branch alone,
\closureA, \closureB\ and \closureC. The slopes at which each subgroup's census
would close exactly are \closingAlphaA, \closingAlphaB\ and \closingAlphaC,
against \closingAlphaAssoc\ for the association aggregate and \closingAlpha\ as
the median over the \closureCells\ individual subgroup cells, all
\closureCellsInside\ of which close inside the carried slope range rather than
requiring an extrapolation. A and B want something close to Salpeter; C wants
something distinctly shallower.

We therefore do \emph{not} claim that a single power law is validated uniformly
from 2 to 120\,$M_\odot$. What is validated is that the association-wide
extrapolation closes near Salpeter, and what is exposed is that imposing one
global slope forces the same $\alpha$ on three subgroups that do not want it.
C's excess is now the fourth independent appearance of a ``Cyg~OB2-C is
different'' signal, alongside its shallower preferred slope, its
family-dependent age and its behaviour in the multiplicity test; the leading
candidate remains distance contamination, which would inflate masses and
therefore closure ratios in exactly C's direction. We report it as an open
science question rather than absorbing it.

\subsection{A branch-resolved supernova history}\label{sec:ledger-result}

Under the permissive all-explode prescription, the baseline branch gives

\begin{equation}
N_{\rm SN} = \NSN \quad (\text{median } \NSNmedian,\ 68\%\ [\NSNlo, \NSNhi]),
\end{equation}

comprising \NSNA\ from Cyg~OB2-A, \NSNB\ from Cyg~OB2-B and exactly \NSNC\ from
Cyg~OB2-C, whose turnoff on this branch has not yet fallen below the IMF's upper
limit. Within the same prescription, the probability that at least one
supernova has occurred is \Pone; the
probability that the most recent falls within the last 100\,kyr is \Precent, and
the median time since it is \tlast\,kyr. The first explosion was \firstSN\,Myr
ago and the rate has been roughly flat at $\rateNow$\,Myr$^{-1}$ since
(Fig.~\ref{fig:history}).

\textbf{The number is not \NSN.} Across the \NSNheadbranches\ headline
all-explode branches
the total spans \NSNheadlo--\NSNheadhi, a factor of \NSNheadfactor; including
$\alpha = 2.6$ it reaches down to \NSNalllo, a factor of \NSNallfactor. The
recent-event probability likewise spans \Precentheadlo--\Precentheadhi. The
branch spread \emph{is} the uncertainty, and it exceeds the Poisson interval of
any single branch. We quote the baseline value with its carried range beside it
and never an average across branches, which for a set that carries no
probability weights would not be a posterior summary.

\subsection{What is robust about the explosion history}\label{sec:explodability}

\paragraph{The whole budget lies in the very-massive regime.}
Every star that has died in Cygnus~OB2 is very massive. Across the grid the
lowest progenitor that explodes on any branch is $\progenitorFloorLo\,M_\odot$,
and the lowest turnoff any star is compared against on a coeval branch is
$\minTurnoffCoeval\,M_\odot$. Consequently, \textbf{for any direct-collapse
threshold at or below $\bhSafeCut\,M_\odot$ the ledger returns exactly zero
supernovae, on every branch and in every iteration}. The interleaved pattern of
explodable and non-explodable ZAMS masses that \citet{sukhbold2016} and
\citet{ertl2016} find between roughly 15 and 25\,$M_\odot$ is \emph{irrelevant}
here, because nothing in that range has had time to die.

This is not a marginal systematic but the shape of the result: the stellar
census measures the deaths available to produce supernovae, while the explosion
ledger conditions on whether stars well above $\bhSafeCut\,M_\odot$ explode at
all (Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). The same separation will matter in other young
associations whose turnoff remains in this uncertain very-massive regime.

\paragraph{What survives the branch structure.}
Two statements survive every all-explode branch: the model-branch spread exceeds
the stochastic interval of any single branch, and every inferred death sits
above the canonical core-collapse regime. A non-zero explosion count does
\emph{not} survive the explodability branches: the hard cutoff can reduce it to
zero. The independent pulsar supplies the complementary fact that, under the
association hypothesis, nature did not choose an explosion-free history.
Together these results require at least one of three physical resolutions: a
successful explosion in the very-massive regime, an older progenitor population,
or binary stripping of a lower-mass progenitor. The count itself, its recency,
and --- as Sect.~\ref{sec:cocoon} shows --- its relevance to the cocoon remain
conditional.

\subsection{Independent evidence for past explosions}\label{sec:crosschecks}

Every comparison in this subsection was pre-registered, with its statistic and
pass condition fixed, before the ledger existed. All five predictions passed.

\paragraph{PSR~J2032+4127 excludes an explosion-free history.}
The pulsar's Be-star companion MT91~213 is \textit{our own census star}
(\textit{Gaia}~DR3~2067835682818358400), a B0V of 17\,$M_\odot$ lying
$0\fdg115$ from the Cyg~OB2-A centroid and already counted in the living census.
A neutron star requires a \emph{successful} explosion. If the association is
accepted, the pulsar therefore \textbf{independently excludes an entirely
explosion-free history}: the ledger gives $P(\geq 1\,{\rm SN}) = \pulsarPone$ if
massive stars explode, against exactly $\pulsarIslands$ under a hard
direct-collapse cutoff. Its characteristic age, widened for the line-of-sight
acceleration its decades-long eccentric orbit imposes, gives 151--401\,kyr,
against a ledger probability of \pulsarAge\ that the last supernova falls inside
that window; its peculiar transverse velocity of $27.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ is as low
as a still-bound binary requires.

What the pulsar does \emph{not} establish is that one of the
$>\progenitorFloorLo\,M_\odot$ single-star progenitors this ledger infers was
the star that exploded. Three readings remain degenerate and we do not pretend
otherwise: high-mass explodability, a population older than we fit, or binary
stripping of a lower-mass progenitor. \emph{All three give a non-zero budget},
which is what the argument needs and all that it needs.

\paragraph{$\gamma$~Cygni --- allowed, unsettled.}
The ledger gives $P = \gammaCygni$\% for a supernova within the remnant's
$\sim$7\,kyr age, so a physical association is allowed but not required. Our
association distance of $\ourDistance$\,kpc overlaps the remnant's
1.7--2.6\,kpc range only at its low extreme. We leave this unsettled by design.

\paragraph{Absent remnants --- weak evidence.}
Even at a generous 100\,kyr visibility lifetime and with no cavity, the ledger
predicts only $\snrExpected$ visible remnants. The absence of a catalogued
remnant is therefore consistent with the cavity argument but does not test it.

\paragraph{$^{26}$Al --- a consistency band.}
$^{26}$Al traces both supernova ejecta and Wolf--Rayet winds, so it constrains
the combination and cannot be inverted for a supernova count. It is reported as
a band and never used as a fit. Run forward rather than backward --- and, unlike
everything else in this subsection, post-hoc rather than frozen at the outset
(Sect.~\ref{sec:cosi}) --- the ledger's supernovae alone supply
$\isoAlFracLo$--$\isoAlFracHi$\% of the measured complex-wide $1809$\,keV flux
\citep{martin2009}. The supernova component is a sub-dominant but not negligible
contributor to a measurement that also contains wind $^{26}$Al and neighbouring
populations; it is not the whole of it.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A measured history, not a single supernova number}

The result of this paper is a distribution over histories, not a number. Three
kinds of uncertainty are kept separate throughout and should be read separately.
The \emph{statistical} uncertainty on the normalization is small. The
\emph{stochastic} uncertainty from sampling a discrete population at these
numbers is the 68\% interval [\NSNlo, \NSNhi] quoted with the baseline. The
\emph{model-branch} spread, \NSNheadlo--\NSNheadhi, is larger than either, and
it is not a probability distribution: the branches carry no weights, so their
range is a statement about what we do not know rather than a posterior.

Quoting \NSN\ alone would be defensible only if the branch axes were known to be
unimportant. They are not: $\alpha$ alone moves the answer by a factor of
several, and the age envelope moves it by more.

\subsection{The very-massive-star explodability problem}

The threshold scan of Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} is the primary explodability
result, and its message is that this ledger is a probe of a regime where theory
is least settled. The hard-cutoff branch we carry is exactly that --- a hard
cutoff --- and we have renamed it accordingly throughout; it is not an
implementation of the interleaved explodability structure of
\citet{sukhbold2016} and \citet{ertl2016}, which in any case lies below the
turnoff at these ages and cannot act.

The pulsar bounds this from one side: at least one star did explode. It does not
tell us which, and the physical conclusion remains conditional on the fate of
stars above roughly 30--50\,$M_\odot$.

\subsection{Does one IMF describe all three subgroups?}

Section~\ref{sec:closure-result} showed that they do not want the same slope.
This matters twice over. First, the global-$\alpha$ grid \emph{imposes} a common
slope, so the branch range it produces understates the freedom the data actually
leave. Second, Cyg~OB2-C's excess, its shallower preferred slope, its
family-dependent age and its possible distance contamination are one connected
finding rather than four separate anomalies: all four are what a population
contaminated by a slightly more distant group would look like.

We tested the first point directly by re-running the ledger with each subgroup
assigned the carried slope its own closure prefers --- A and B at $\alpha=2.3$,
C at $\alpha=2.0$ on every family and $R_V$ cell. The mixed-slope ledger spans
\mixedLo--\mixedHi\ supernovae, which lies \emph{inside} the carried headline
range, so relaxing the single-slope assumption does not open a range beyond the
one already reported. But its effect is strongly branch-dependent: on the
\mixedDeadBranches\ branches where C's turnoff sits at the IMF ceiling the
change is at most \mixedShiftDead\%, whereas on the \mixedAliveBranches\ where C
is dynamically alive it reaches \mixedShift\%. The subgroup heterogeneity is
therefore not presentational --- where it acts, it is worth as much as an $R_V$
step. The engine reproduces the stored single-slope ledger to \mixedCheck\%.

\subsection{Application to the Cygnus cocoon}\label{sec:cocoon}

The ledger was measured without reference to any high-energy interpretation of
the region; it can now be confronted with the interpretation that demands the
most of it. Evaluating the requirement of \citet{harer2025} --- an event of the
right age, progenitor type and location --- over the \Pverdictbranches\ headline
branches gives a score

\begin{equation}
S \;=\; C_1 \, C_3 \, C_4 \;=\; \Pverdictlo\!-\!\Pverdicthi
\quad (\text{median } \Pverdictmed).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{What $S$ is, and what it is not.}
$S$ is a \emph{conditional scenario-availability score}, not a calibrated
probability, and we do not present it as one. Only $C_1$, the age term
($\Conelo$--\Conehi), is a sampled Monte-Carlo probability. $C_3$ is a
deterministic mass-to-type mapping. $C_4$ is an upper bound held fixed.
Independence of the three is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the
explosion-energy condition is deliberately not multiplied in, because this
analysis measures progenitor masses and not explosion energies.
Table~\ref{tab:cocoon} states the status of each term and its dominant
limitation.

\paragraph{$C_3$ is a model mapping.}
$C_3 = \Cthree$ exactly, because every simulated progenitor exceeds the adopted
$30\,M_\odot$ stripping threshold. That is a fact about the ledger's mass floor,
not evidence that every explosion would have been observed as a
stripped-envelope event. Whether a star of a given ZAMS mass arrives at collapse
without its hydrogen envelope depends on its wind mass-loss history, which is
metallicity-dependent \citep{vink2001} and whose absolute scale has been revised
downwards substantially by more recent theory \citep{bjorklund2021} and by
observational reassessments of clumping \citep{smith2014}, and on binary
interaction, which affects the majority of massive stars
\citep{sana2012, demink2014}. We therefore report $C_3$ as a bracket rather than
a measurement: moving the threshold to $40\,M_\odot$ gives
$C_3 = \CthreeFortyLo$--\CthreeFortyHi, and an explicitly pessimistic
$60\,M_\odot$ assumption gives $\CthreeSixtyLo$--\CthreeSixtyHi. Under that
pessimistic mapping $S$ spans \scorePessLo--\scorePessHi, and
\scorePessTwoAbove\ of \scorePessTwon\ shallow-slope branches still clear the
0.5 boundary --- so the qualitative split survives, but $C_3 = \Cthree$ does not
survive as a statement about nature.

\paragraph{$C_4$ is a bound, so we show the answer against it.}
$C_4$ is fixed at \Cfour\ from the runaway census. That census is
two-dimensional and footprint-limited, so it is a lower bound on escape and
hence an \emph{upper} bound on the in-situ fraction; and it is measured on
living stars below the turnoff, whereas the quantity required is the escape
fraction of already-dead, more massive progenitors, which need not be the same.
Figure~\ref{fig:score} therefore shows $S$ as a function of $C_4$ rather than at
a point.

Two thresholds must be stated separately, and the distinction is not cosmetic.
$C_4$ below \CfourAny\ moves \emph{at least one} shallow-slope branch below 0.5;
$C_4$ below \CfourAll\ moves \emph{every} one of them below it. In the other
direction, $C_4$ would have to exceed \CfourLift\ before even the highest
$\alpha = 2.3$ branch rose above 0.5 --- so the split between the two slopes is
not an artefact of the adopted $C_4$, though it is not immune to it either.

\paragraph{The score hinges on one axis.}
The spread in $S$ is \spreadAlpha\ across $\alpha$, against \spreadRv\ across
$R_V$, \spreadFamily\ across isochrone family and \spreadDelta\ across
star-formation duration. At the adopted $C_4$ the split is exact: every one of
the \PverdictTwon\ branches at $\alpha = 2.0$ clears 0.5
($\PverdictTwolo$--\PverdictTwohi) and none of the \PverdictTwoThreen\ at
$\alpha = 2.3$ does ($\PverdictTwoThreelo$--\PverdictTwoThreehi), with the
boundary falling between the two slopes. The sensitivity-only $\alpha = 2.6$
branches give $S = \Pverdictsixlo$--\Pverdictsixhi.

\paragraph{The split, restricted to validated fits.}
The headline result --- every $\alpha = 2.0$ branch above 0.5, no $\alpha = 2.3$
branch above it --- uses branches that include failed mass-function cells
(Sect.~\ref{sec:massfunction}). Restricting to the \gateStrictBranches\ branches
built entirely on cells that passed, the split survives: \strictTwoAbove\ of
\strictTwon\ remaining $\alpha = 2.0$ branches stay above 0.5
($\strictTwolo$--\strictTwohi) and \strictTwoThreeAbove\ of \strictTwoThreen\
remaining $\alpha = 2.3$ branches rise above it. The qualitative conclusion is
robust; the branch representation is badly imbalanced, and the ``all versus
none'' phrasing should be read with that in mind.

\paragraph{What we cannot say.}
The analysis estimates the \emph{availability} of a suitable event from
Cygnus~OB2. It cannot quantify whether Cygnus~OB2, rather than a neighbouring
Cygnus population, was the source of an event somewhere in the larger cavity. We
attempted a coarse literature-based budget for the neighbouring associations
from the tabulation of \citet{martin2010} and \citet{knodlseder2002}, with a
pre-registered validation test requiring that the same coarse estimator reproduce our own
measured ledger to within an order of magnitude when applied to their Cyg~OB2
row. \textbf{That test failed}, and we report it as failed: at the 2.5\,Myr age
those authors adopt for Cygnus~OB2 the turnoff lies above the IMF ceiling and
the coarse estimator returns identically zero against our measured \NSN. Run
instead at the age we measure it overshoots by a factor
\coarseRatioLo--\coarseRatioHi. The estimator is dominated by the assumed age,
which for the neighbours is exactly the quantity no homogeneous census yet
constrains. We therefore decline to publish a neighbour budget rather than
publish a number rejected by its own validation test, and the cocoon score is to be read as
Cygnus~OB2's availability alone. Appendix~\ref{app:external} records the
attempt.

\paragraph{The framing rule.}
The rule --- fixed at planning time and operationalized before the number was
known --- returns \textsc{inconclusive}, and hence this article rather than a
Letter. Under the model's own permissive ``few hundred kyr'' allowance every
branch clears 0.5 ($S = \Ppermlo$--\Ppermhi). The scenario is therefore
\emph{available} against its authors' permissive statement and \emph{unresolved}
against their preferred $\sim$50\,kyr. Both windows were pre-registered before
either was computed.

\paragraph{Near one half is not uninformative.}
The score changes strongly with the physically specified time window: it is
0.055 for 7\,kyr, \Precent\ for 100\,kyr and \Ppermhi\ for the permissive
window. A last supernova distributed uniformly over the association's \firstSN\,Myr
active period would fall inside 100\,kyr with probability \ignorance; we measure
\Precent, a factor of \measurementFactor\ higher, because the rate is measured
rather than assumed.

\subsection{Binary mass transfer: a conservative sensitivity bracket}
\label{sec:binary}

Our count is a single-star turnoff calculation, and most massive stars have
companions --- \citet{sana2012} find a close-binary fraction near 0.7 for
15--60\,$M_\odot$, and about two-thirds of Cygnus~OB2's own O and early-B stars
are resolved or spectroscopic multiples. Two independent lines indicate what
that omission is worth.

Empirically, the BPASS \citep{stanway2018} calculation of \citet{harer2025} ---
\emph{with} binaries, for a population of the same mass and IMF slope --- gives a
core-collapse rate at 4\,Myr that is \bpassRatioLo--\bpassRatioHi\ times ours
once the difference in IMF integration ranges is corrected: the binary-inclusive
rate sits \emph{below} our single-star one. Theoretically,
\citet{zapartas2017} find that binarity raises the integrated number of
core-collapse supernovae by $14^{+15}_{-14}$\%, but attribute the increase to
\emph{late} events 50--200\,Myr after birth from 4--8\,$M_\odot$ binaries, and
find the early delay-time distributions ``remarkably similar'', diverging only
around 20\,Myr. Cygnus~OB2 is 4\,Myr old, so the entire ledger sits inside the
regime where the two distributions agree.

Adopting a deliberately conservative $\pm\binaryBracket$\% bracket --- the top of
the \citet{zapartas2017} enhancement applied in full despite its channel being
inapplicable --- and running it through the ledger engine moves the baseline from
\binaryLo\ to \binaryHi\ supernovae, a span of \binarySpan\ against a headline
branch span of \branchSpan. \textbf{This is a scenario bracket, not a formal
statistical bound on binary evolution at 4\,Myr}, and we do not claim the
unmodelled binary systematic has been bounded in a strict probabilistic sense.
What it does establish, and what matters here, is that the tested bracket is
about \binaryRatio\ times smaller than the model-branch spread we already
report.

Nor does the living binary fraction translate directly. That
$30^{+10}_{-15}$\% of massive main-sequence stars are binary products
\citep{demink2014} is a statement about survivors: accretors and mergers are
rejuvenated, so a star pushed above the present turnoff by mass transfer has had
its clock partly reset and has typically \emph{not} yet exploded.

\subsection{Systematics budget}\label{sec:systematics}

\paragraph{The absolute extinction scale.}
The anchor-versus-photometry offset of Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} is a
systematic on the mass \emph{scale}: it moves all three subgroups together and
does not produce the A/B/C ordering. Everything that scales with mass ---
the normalizations $k = \kA$, \kB\ and \kC, the turnoff masses \turnoffA,
\turnoffB\ and \turnoffC\,$M_\odot$, and $N_{\rm SN}$ --- inherits it.

\paragraph{Cyg~OB2-B's age is a one-sided bound.}
\railB\% of B's counts-based age posterior sits on the topmost of its nine prior
nodes. A posterior that piles up against the edge of its prior grid reports a
bound, not a measurement, and the direction is known: were B's true age the top
node (\railtopB\,Myr) its supernova count would be higher still. B's
contribution to $N_{\rm SN}$ is a lower bound at the precision quoted.

\paragraph{The in-situ fraction is an upper bound.}
Of the living census, \livingRetained\ stars above 8\,$M_\odot$ were retained in
the association and at most \runawayFraction\% of the ledger's supernovae were
not in situ. Because the runaway count is a lower bound
(Sect.~\ref{sec:runaways}), the true escaped fraction is higher and $C_4$
correspondingly lower --- a direction that works against the cocoon scenario,
quantified in Fig.~\ref{fig:score}.

\paragraph{The age dependence, disclosed rather than absorbed.}
Below \ageZeroBelow\,Myr the turnoff has not crossed the IMF ceiling and nothing
has died; above it the count rises steeply, reaching \NSNatSix\ supernovae at
6\,Myr (Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). Every number in this paper inherits the
ages of Sect.~\ref{sec:ages}, and that is the sensitivity.

\paragraph{Where the deaths sit.}
Across the whole grid the lowest turnoff any star is compared against is
$\minTurnoffAll\,M_\odot$ and the lowest exploding progenitor is
$\minProgenitor\,M_\odot$, reached only on the widest star-formation-window
branches, where up to \fracBelowFiftyTwo\% of the supernovae come from
progenitors below $52\,M_\odot$. On the baseline and on every coeval branch the
floor is higher.

\paragraph{Association mass, and which one.}
Three nested integrals of the same normalization can all be called ``the
association mass'', and they differ by a factor of 1.67. We quote the
multiplicity-adjusted stellar mass over 0.08--120\,$M_\odot$,
$\massTotal\times10^{4}\,M_\odot$. The like-for-like partner of
\citeauthor{wright2015}'s \citeyear{wright2015} $1.65\times10^{4}\,M_\odot$ ---
primary stars over the full range --- is $\massPrimaries\times10^{4}\,M_\odot$,
a factor \massVsWright. Integrating only 0.5--120\,$M_\odot$ instead gives
$\massPrimariesHalf\times10^{4}\,M_\odot$, which coincides with the literature
value but compares a different quantity. The comparison is an agreement at the
factor level, which is the resolution IMF-extrapolated cluster masses admit.

\subsection{Pre-registration as part of the result}

Every marker comparison in Sect.~\ref{sec:crosschecks} was pre-registered, with
its statistic and pass condition fixed, before the ledger it tests existed; the
markers themselves were frozen at data acquisition. The question ``did you tune
the analysis to the markers?'' therefore has a checkable answer rather than a
reassurance. The same discipline produced results we would rather not have:
predictions recorded as failed and not reinterpreted, two intermediate results
withdrawn after defects were found by comparison against externally known
values, and the neighbouring-population validation test of Sect.~\ref{sec:cocoon}
(Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}). We report this not as virtue but because the
score's credibility rests on it: an analysis that can be tuned to a
$\sim$50\,kyr window is not evidence about a $\sim$50\,kyr window.

\subsection{Radioactive-isotope consistency and forecast}\label{sec:cosi}

\emph{Unlike the cross-checks of Sect.~\ref{sec:crosschecks}, this comparison
was not frozen before the analysis began: it is a post-hoc addition, chosen
after the ledger existed and pre-registered before it was scored.} We flag it
because the weight the frozen markers carry depends on that distinction.

$^{60}$Fe is the supernova-specific tracer --- Wolf--Rayet winds make none of it,
so unlike $^{26}$Al it counts explosions rather than a combination. Carrying
published per-supernova yields as a branch fixed before any flux was computed,
and accumulating each event's ejecta at its own progenitor mass with decay from
its own epoch, the headline branches predict
$\isoFeLo$--$\isoFeHi\times10^{-3}\,M_\odot$ of $^{60}$Fe surviving now, beside
$\isoAlLo$--$\isoAlHi\times10^{-3}\,M_\odot$ of supernova $^{26}$Al. The
association is nowhere near steady state --- only a fraction
$\isoSatFeLo$--$\isoSatFeHi$ of the saturated $^{60}$Fe mass has accumulated ---
so the usual rate-times-lifetime estimate would overstate the signal
several-fold.

\textbf{A COSI measurement or upper limit would constrain the combination of
high-mass IMF slope and very-massive-star yield and explodability}, not $\alpha$
alone. That joint statement is the honest form of the forecast, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:isotopes} shows why: the spread between published yield arms is a
factor $\isoArmSpread$ in the predicted flux, against $\isoBranchSpread$ across
the entire headline branch set \citep{limongi2006}. Within the primary arm the
prediction is nevertheless clean: at $\ourDistance$\,kpc the median $1173$\,keV
flux is $\isoFluxFe\times10^{-6}$\,ph\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ against COSI's
expected $3\sigma$ narrow-line sensitivity of $\isoCosi\times10^{-6}$ in a
two-year survey \citep{tomsick2023}, and $\isoCosiTwo$ of $\isoCosiTwon$
branches at $\alpha = 2.0$ clear it while $\isoCosiTwoThree$ of
$\isoCosiTwoThreen$ at $\alpha = 2.3$ do, a factor $\isoAlphaRatio$ between the
medians.

Two things bound that claim and we give them equal weight. The current standard
compilation predicts nothing at all: \citet{limongi2018}'s recommended scenario
collapses every star above $25\,M_\odot$ straight to a black hole, and
$\isoNullBelow$ of the $\isoNullSampled$ million supernovae we sample fall below
that threshold, so on that prescription this ledger's $^{60}$Fe yield is
identically zero \citep{falla2025}. A non-detection would then favour precisely
the explodability that PSR~J2032+4127 disfavours, and that tension would itself
be the result. And existing data are already near the boundary: our richest
branch predicts a combined $^{60}$Fe flux within $\isoSpiMargin$\% of the
INTEGRAL/SPI upper limit for the Cygnus region,
$\isoSpiLimit\times10^{-5}$\,ph\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ \citep{martin2009}.

The forecast is $^{60}$Fe-rich by Galactic standards --- the predicted
$^{60}$Fe/$^{26}$Al flux ratio is $\isoRatioLo$--$\isoRatioHi$, against
$\isoRatioGal \pm \isoRatioGalErr$ measured for the Galaxy \citep{wang2020} ---
but the two are not the same quantity, and the difference is expected rather
than anomalous: our $^{26}$Al denominator counts supernova ejecta only, where
the Galactic ratio's includes wind $^{26}$Al, and every progenitor here lies
above $30\,M_\odot$, where the $^{60}$Fe yield peaks.

\subsection{What \textit{Gaia}~DR4 will and will not resolve}

DR4 improves three things that matter here: radial velocities for bright OB
stars make the traceback three-dimensional, so runaways stop being a lower bound
and $C_4$ tightens; better astrometry and photometry sharpen the subgroup ages,
including Cyg~OB2-C's family-dependent ambiguity and the 0-to-several supernova
range that follows from it; and deeper membership assigns subgroups to the
currently unlabelled massive members and orphan anchors. \textit{Gaia}~XP
spectra address Cyg~OB2-B's anchor famine specifically. A parallax-blind
membership rerun also becomes far more informative at DR4 precision.

\textbf{DR4 will not settle the dominant term.} The score hinges on the
high-mass IMF slope, with a spread of \spreadAlpha\ against \spreadFamily\ for
isochrone family, \spreadRv\ for $R_V$ and \spreadDelta\ for star-formation
duration. Distinguishing $\alpha = 2.0$ from $2.3$ needs a larger or deeper
massive-star census, or a second association analysed identically --- not better
astrometry on this one.

\subsection{Limitations and reproducibility}\label{sec:limitations}

Three formal validation tests failed and are recorded as failed rather than
reinterpreted.
The ledger's pre-registered \emph{relative} convergence criterion failed; the
stored execution record carries that verdict unchanged, and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} reports the absolute drift of material cells, which
demonstrates numerical stability without rewriting the criterion as a pass. One
pre-registered ledger prediction about the mass floor failed and caught a real
over-generalization. And the neighbouring-population validation test of
Sect.~\ref{sec:cocoon}
failed, with the consequence stated there.

Three questions are named and left for later work rather than held against this
paper: a parallax-blind membership test, which is the only way to break the
circularity in the two-distance non-confirmation; a distance-contamination test
for Cyg~OB2-C; and a quantitative neighbouring-association budget, which needs a
homogeneous census of those associations rather than a coarser method applied to
2002-era literature counts.

Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} gives the reproducibility map: the versioned input
list, the script-to-output map, the validation records, the superseded artifacts and
why they are forbidden, the environment, and the command sequence that
regenerates every number, table and figure in this paper.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Three stable kinematic subgroups are found within the selected Cygnus~OB2
      member sample, at $\ourDistance$\,kpc. There is no evidence for two
      distinct distances \emph{among the members we select}; because parallax
      was a selection feature, this does not exclude a foreground population.
\item Differential extinction is spatially coherent and cannot be represented by
      a single regional value, and the subgroup age structure --- \ageA, \ageB\
      and \ageC\,Myr, two older and one younger --- materially changes the
      high-mass history. Cyg~OB2-B's age rails against its prior grid, so its
      supernova contribution is a one-sided lower bound.
\item The baseline supernova ledger is $N_{\rm SN} = \NSN$ (median \NSNmedian,
      68\% [\NSNlo, \NSNhi]) under the all-explode prescription, but the honest
      conditional result is the carried range \NSNheadlo--\NSNheadhi\ across
      \NSNheadbranches\ all-explode branches. The
      model-branch spread exceeds the stochastic interval of any one branch.
\item All inferred deaths occupy the uncertain very-high-mass explodability
      regime: for any direct-collapse threshold at or below $\bhSafeCut\,M_\odot$
      the ledger returns exactly zero, so the result is conditional on whether
      very massive stars explode, and the 15--25\,$M_\odot$ island structure is
      irrelevant at this age.
\item PSR~J2032+4127 independently excludes an entirely explosion-free history,
      if its association with Cygnus~OB2 is accepted. Combined with the hard-
      cutoff result, this requires either a successful very-massive-star
      explosion, an older progenitor population, or a binary-modified lower-mass
      progenitor; it does not identify which channel occurred.
\item Association-wide closure is near Salpeter-like --- \closureMedianRatios\ as
      a median of subgroup ratios, \closureWeighted\ star-weighted --- but the
      subgroups are heterogeneous, with closing slopes \closingAlphaA,
      \closingAlphaB\ and \closingAlphaC. A single universal slope from 2 to
      120\,$M_\odot$ cannot be claimed without that qualification.
\item The cocoon application is a conditional scenario-availability score
      ($S = \Pverdictlo$--\Pverdicthi), not a calibrated probability. It is
      dominated by the IMF slope and rests on a deterministic type mapping and
      an in-situ upper bound. Whether Cygnus~OB2 rather than a neighbour
      supplied an event elsewhere in the cavity is outside what these data
      constrain.
\item \textit{Gaia}~DR4 will improve ages, distance contamination and
      three-dimensional runaways; it will not by itself determine the high-mass
      IMF.
\item COSI can constrain the combined IMF, yield and explodability problem, with
      a clean split by slope on one specified yield arm.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Membership validation and the distance-selection
limitation}\label{app:membership}

\paragraph{Automatic versus manual recovery.}
The recall benchmark against \citet{berlanas2019} decomposes as follows. Their
published list contains \recallPublished\ members. Of these, \recallAnalyzable\
fall inside our quality-analyzable sample, and the automatic full-covariance
membership posterior recovers \recallAuto\ of them, or \recallAutoPct\%. A
further \recallManual\ are retained as manual spectroscopic quality exceptions
--- stars excluded by the astrometric quality criteria, chiefly on RUWE or
absence from the query, but carrying independent spectroscopic evidence of
membership. Total recall is thus \recallTotal\ of \recallPublished, or
\recallTotalPct\%. The pre-specified acceptance criterion was set on the total,
and the total meets it; the
automatic fraction is the number to quote when the question is how well the
classifier performs unaided. The adopted footprint is itself taken from
\citet{berlanas2019}, so the spatial component of this benchmark is not
independent of it either.

\paragraph{Why the catalogue boundary is 0.5.}
For a calibrated two-class posterior, $P>0.5$ is the maximum-posterior decision
rule under equal costs for admitting a field star and rejecting a member. It is
therefore statistically transparent, but it is not a field-wide astrophysical
standard: different catalogues should choose different boundaries when their
science goals assign different costs to completeness and contamination. As a
descriptive revision check, thresholds of 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 retain
\thresholdMembersLow, \thresholdMembersAdopted\ and \thresholdMembersHigh\ clean
automatic members, respectively. External-list recovery changes only from
\thresholdRecallLow\% to \thresholdRecallHigh\% across the full interval, while
the mean control-to-target yield decreases from \thresholdControlLow\% to
\thresholdControlHigh\%. The adopted boundary is thus a reproducible central
handoff, and the reported trend makes the alternative purity--completeness
trade-off explicit; it is not justified by an appeal to convention.

\paragraph{Control fields and prior calibration.}
Three control fields at matched Galactic latitude were processed through the
identical selection, clustering and membership model, and their aggregate yield
above $P > 0.5$ is \controlYield\% of the association field's after area
normalization. Because the same control fields were used to calibrate the
mixture prior, this is an internal consistency check of high value and not a
fully external false-positive rate, and Sect.~\ref{sec:membership-result} says
so where it quotes the number.

\paragraph{Subgroup seed stability.}
Gaussian mixtures were fitted for $k = 2\ldots8$ over 50 deterministic seeds and
accepted on the adjusted Rand index between seeds rather than on an information
criterion. Only $k = 3$ is stable. Per-star label confusion over those seeds is
below a fraction of a per cent, and a refit of the entire branch grid under
stable labels moves the worst baseline residual by less than the Monte-Carlo
floor, which excludes mislabelling as a source of the mass-function structure.

\paragraph{Why the distance test is conditional.}
The one- versus two-component distance comparison was run on the selected
members and prefers one component. The logical structure is the issue, not the
statistics: parallax entered the membership mixture as a feature, so a genuine
foreground group at a distinct distance would have received low membership
probability and been removed \emph{before} the distance test could see it. The
test therefore constrains the depth of the sample it was given, which is what we
claim, and cannot address the existence of the foreground population, which we
do not claim. Breaking the circularity requires re-deriving membership without
parallax and then re-running the entire downstream chain on the resulting
sample; at DR3 parallax precision, and with an intrinsic depth of order 45\,pc
at $\ourDistance$\,kpc, that rerun would be dominated by measurement error, which
is why it is deferred to DR4 rather than attempted here.

\section{Extinction, age and mass-function diagnostics}\label{app:diagnostics}

\paragraph{Anchor distribution and kriging support.}
The spectroscopic anchors are distributed very unevenly between the subgroups.
Cyg~OB2-B contains 4 anchors inside the member sample against 59 for A and 42
for C, and its eighth-nearest anchor lies $0\fdg374$ away against $0\fdg089$ and
$0\fdg139$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:extinction}, centre). The kriging weights sum to 1.000
for A and 0.992 for C but only 0.772 for B. This is an observational limitation
of the DR3-era anchor set, not a modelling choice, and it is why the repaired
prior moved B and left A and C unchanged: no per-subgroup decision was taken.
The same fact surfaces three times --- as B's extinction correction, as the last
mass-function fit limitation, and as B's age railing against its prior support.

\paragraph{The anchor-versus-photometry offset.}
At matched sky position, prior-free broadband photometry prefers an $A_V$ about
0.5\,mag below the spectroscopically calibrated anchors, at high significance.
The absolute extinction scale, and hence the mass scale, the normalization and
$N_{\rm SN}$, rests on the anchor calibration rather than on the photometry
alone. This is a systematic on the \emph{scale}, not on the relative structure:
it moves all three subgroups together and does not produce the A/B/C ordering.
Its sign is the reassuring one --- the anchors read \emph{higher}, the opposite
of the failure mode in which a selection-biased anchor set would drag the scale
down.

\paragraph{Excluded and railed age branches.}
Of the age branches attempted, a minority are excluded outright for
insufficient stars or an unmeasurable indicator, and a further set rail against
the edge of the age grid. Both classes are recorded with their exclusion reason
rather than silently dropped. Cyg~OB2-B's PMS indicator in particular remains
unmeasurable, so the documented disagreement between its two age indicators is
reported rather than resolved.

\paragraph{The residual map and the fit-quality definition.}
A cell passes the residual-fit criterion only if its calibration-window $\chi^2$
probability is at least 0.01, its residual trend probability at least 0.05, and
its worst absolute Pearson residual at most 3.0. Figure~\ref{fig:massfunction}
shows all \gateCells\ cells with those verdicts. The failure counts quoted in
Sect.~\ref{sec:massfunction} are those of the final chain, which is the chain
every downstream number was computed on; an earlier accepted version of the
normalization gives the same \gatePassing\ of \gateCells\ total but a different
distribution of \emph{which} cells fail, and the final chain is authoritative
here.

\section{Ledger algorithm and convergence}\label{app:ledger}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
For each branch: draw a paired $(k, \mathrm{age})$ sample from the joint
posterior; draw a star-formation duration offset within $\pm\delta/2$; compute
the lowest turnoff over the birth window; draw a Poisson number of stars above
it with mean $k$ times the IMF integral; sample their masses from the truncated
IMF; mark as dead those whose mass exceeds the turnoff at their own birth time;
convert each death to an epoch by inverting the same turnoff relation for the
stellar lifetime; and apply the explodability filter. Because the lifetime is
the inverse of the turnoff relation used for the ages, the ledger cannot
disagree with the age fit about when a star of a given mass dies.

\paragraph{The pre-registered convergence test failed.}
The pre-registered convergence criterion was \emph{relative} and it
\textbf{failed}: the stored execution record carries the failed verdict, and it
has not been reinterpreted. The failure was driven by the criterion's own
specification rather than by numerical instability --- a relative tolerance
around cells whose value is zero or near zero cannot be met by any number of
iterations, and the ledger contains many such cells by construction, since whole
branches return exactly zero. Reported instead, and \emph{in addition} rather
than in substitution, is the absolute drift of material cells under a doubling
of the iteration count, which is small. The honest summary is that the analysis
is numerically stable and that the criterion as written was not a good test of that,
and the record retains both statements.

\paragraph{The threshold scan.}
The direct-collapse scan reruns the same populations under a hard cutoff at each
of a series of threshold masses, filtering the same draws rather than
re-sampling, so the scan and the baseline share their Monte-Carlo realization.

\section{External checks and the conditional cocoon
calculation}\label{app:external}

\paragraph{Pre-registered predictions.}
All five pre-registered marker predictions passed and their statistics and pass conditions
were fixed before the ledger existed. They are reported in
Sect.~\ref{sec:crosschecks} with their outcomes unchanged.

\paragraph{Pulsar assumptions.}
The pulsar argument assumes that PSR~J2032+4127 is associated with Cygnus~OB2,
which the identification of its companion with a star in our own census
supports but does not prove; that a neutron star requires a successful
explosion; and that the characteristic age, widened for orbital acceleration,
brackets the true age. Three interpretations remain degenerate --- high-mass
explodability, an older population, or binary stripping of a lower-mass
progenitor --- and all three give a non-zero budget.

\paragraph{The three cocoon terms.}
Table~\ref{tab:cocoon} states what each of $C_1$, $C_3$ and $C_4$ is, how it is
treated numerically, and its dominant limitation. The essential points are that
only $C_1$ is a sampled probability; that $C_3$ returns unity because the
ledger's mass floor lies above the adopted threshold and not because nature
guarantees stripping; and that $C_4$ is an upper bound applied as a constant,
whose sensitivity is given in Sect.~\ref{sec:cocoon} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:score}.

\paragraph{The neighbouring-association budget, attempted and withdrawn.}
The original plan called for a coarse literature-based supernova budget for the
neighbouring Cygnus populations. We attempted it, from the tabulation of
\citet{martin2010} after \citet{knodlseder2002}, which gives for each of
\neighbourPopulations\ populations an observed massive-star count in a stated
mass interval together with a distance and an age. The method normalizes a power
law to that count truncated at the turnoff, integrates above the turnoff for the
cumulative deaths, and differences over 100\,kyr for the recent rate, using this
project's own turnoff relation so that only the inputs differ from the measured
ledger.

The pre-registered validation test required the same estimator, applied to those authors'
own Cyg~OB2 row, to reproduce our measured ledger to within an order of
magnitude. It \textbf{failed}. At their adopted 2.5\,Myr the turnoff lies above
the IMF ceiling and the estimator returns identically zero against our measured
\NSN\ --- and run at the age we measure, it overshoots by a factor
\coarseRatioLo--\coarseRatioHi. For reference, the measured ledger expects
\coarseRecent\ supernovae in the last 100\,kyr on the baseline branch, which is
the quantity a neighbour budget would have had to be compared against. Both
failures have the same cause: the estimator
is dominated by the assumed age, and heterogeneous literature ages for the
neighbours are precisely what no homogeneous census yet supplies. Per the
pre-registration, the budget is therefore not reported as a number and the
cavity-source question is scoped out of the paper's claims. The attempt, its
inputs and its failed validation record are retained in the released records so that the
decision can be audited rather than taken on trust.

\paragraph{Frozen evidence versus post-hoc forecast.}
The independent markers were frozen at data acquisition, before the normalization, the
census and the ledger existed. The $^{60}$Fe forecast of Sect.~\ref{sec:cosi}
was not: it was chosen after the ledger existed and pre-registered before it was
scored. These are different kinds of evidence and the text distinguishes them
wherever it uses them.

\section{Provenance and reproducibility}\label{app:provenance}

Every cut, threshold, catalogue version and query is recorded with its date in
the project's provenance log, and every validation verdict exists as a
machine-readable record. Nothing is overwritten: superseded artifacts are
retained with version suffixes and stay referenced, and the released input
resolver refuses the superseded products by name so that a stale table cannot
reach the manuscript even by accident. The manuscript's numbers are generated
directly from those artifacts; no number in this paper was typed by hand, and a
validator fails the build on any bare number in running text, on any macro that
stops being used, and on any reference to a forbidden input.

Three caveats belong here rather than in a README. First, the final authorized
input manifest matches the files on disk, but several older manifests from the
early analysis stages describe superseded states and are retained as historical
records rather than as current checksums; they are labelled accordingly. Second,
the pre-registration for the post-hoc isotope forecast was a timestamped local
specification written shortly before the scoring execution, with its prior
qualitative expectations explicitly disclosed. It was \emph{not} committed
before execution and it carried no input hashes, and we describe it as what it
was rather than claiming a stronger status; the revision analysis that produced
the fit-quality landscape, the closure slopes, the scenario-score sensitivities and the
neighbour-budget attempt does carry hashed inputs and was written before any of
its results existed. Third, the released tree contains the code and the
versioned intermediate tables; the bulk survey working tree is regenerated from
the recorded archival queries.

\section{Withdrawn and failed results}\label{app:withdrawn}

A chain that reports only its successes cannot be audited. The following are
retained in the released records and must not be quoted as results.

\paragraph{Withdrawn intermediate results.}
A runaway census computed from absolute rather than peculiar proper motions,
which measured the association's bulk drift rather than ejection and was
invalidated by comparison against a canonical ejected star. A set of closure
ratios whose forward integral was truncated at the same threshold its response
scatters across, which absorbed most of an apparent excess. And an $^{26}$Al
ejecta mass whose denominator was wrong by about two orders of magnitude; the
corrected comparison is quoted in flux space in Sect.~\ref{sec:crosschecks},
where no mass conversion of the measurement is needed, and the erroneous value

\paragraph{Failed pre-registered predictions.}
The ledger's relative convergence criterion (Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}); one
ledger prediction about the mass floor, which failed and caught a genuine
over-generalization about the minimum progenitor mass on the widest
star-formation-window branches; one isotope prediction that passed only
vacuously and whose corrected form failed; and the neighbouring-population
validation test of
Appendix~\ref{app:external}. Each is recorded with its original wording and its
outcome. None was reinterpreted after the result was seen.

\paragraph{Defects found and fixed within the revision analysis.}
Two coding defects were found during the revision analysis itself, both by
comparison against an independently computed quantity rather than by inspection:
an inverted monotonicity assumption in the closing-slope inversion, and an
inverted numerator and denominator in the star-weighted association closure.
Both were fixed before any result was reported, neither affects an upstream
number, and both are recorded in the released execution records.
